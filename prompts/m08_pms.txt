## CONTEXT & OBJECTIVE

The Performance Management System (PMS) translates company values and KPIs into individual role 
accountability. It answers:
- How are people evaluated? (On what criteria?)
- How is performance rated? (Scale? Process?)
- How are KPIs and values weighted?
- How are decisions made around compensation, promotion, and separation?

A well-designed PMS:
- **Links to Values** (M01): Behavioral indicators are observable and tied to company values
- **Links to KPIs** (M05): Role-specific KPIs are clear and cascaded from company KPIs
- **Fair & Transparent**: Everyone knows what success looks like
- **Development-Focused**: Creates growth opportunities, not just scorecards
- **Indian-Context**: Aligns with labor laws, cultural expectations

## GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS

### 1. REVIEW CYCLE RECOMMENDATION

Define when and how often people are evaluated.

**Typical Cadences** (pick one):

| Cadence | Frequency | Best For | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual Review | 1x per year (Apr-May) | Stable companies, traditional culture | Single review; can miss mid-year issues |
| Semi-Annual | 2x per year (Sep, Apr) | Growth companies, high-accountability culture | Catches performance issues early |
| Quarterly | 4x per year | Fast-growing startups, high-stakes roles | Keeps pulse; can feel heavy in admin |

**Recommendation**: For most Indian companies: **Semi-annual reviews (Sep and Apr)** balance 
development feedback with reasonable admin burden.

**Calendar** (April-March fiscal year):

- **June-July**: Mid-year 360 feedback collection
- **Early August**: Mid-year calibration meeting
- **Late August-Sep**: Mid-year review conversations (1:1s)
- **November-December**: Annual planning; year-end preparation
- **January-February**: Year-end feedback collection
- **Early March**: Year-end calibration meeting
- **Late March-Apr**: Year-end review conversations; compensation decisions

---

### 2. RATING SCALE (5-Point Recommended for India)

Define the rating scale and what each level means.

**Recommended Scale** (5-point for Indian context):

| Rating | Label | Definition | % of Population | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Exceptional / Far Exceeds | Significantly exceeds role expectations; role model | 5-10% | Promotion ready; highest raises/bonuses |
| 4 | Exceeds | Exceeds role expectations consistently | 20-30% | Core high performers; raises/bonuses |
| 3 | Meets | Meets role expectations; solid contributor | 40-50% | Core team; standard raises; growth opportunity |
| 2 | Below Expectations | Does not consistently meet expectations | 5-10% | Improvement plan required; escalation path |
| 1 | Unsatisfactory | Well below expectations; performance issue | <5% | Termination discussion or major restructuring |

**Notes**:
- Distribution should approximate above %s (bell curve)
- Rating 2 + triggers formal improvement plan (PIP)
- Rating 1 is termination path (requires HR + legal review in India)
- Rating 3 is "healthy" - not every employee needs to be exceptional

---

### 3. KPI WEIGHT vs. BEHAVIORAL WEIGHT

Define the ratio of KPI-based evaluation vs. values-based evaluation.

**Typical Splits** (by role and level):

| Role Level | KPI Weight | Behavioral Weight | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Individual Contributor** (IC) | 60-70% | 30-40% | Direct execution drives results |
| **Team Lead / Manager** | 50-60% | 40-50% | Balance results + team leadership |
| **Director / Head of Function** | 40-50% | 50-60% | Strategy, culture, and team development |
| **CEO** | 40% | 60% | Vision, values, culture ownership |

**Recommendation**: Start with **60/40 (KPI/Behavioral)** for most roles; adjust by level.

**Examples**:

- **Sales Representative**: 70% KPI (revenue target), 30% Behavioral (customer respect, teamwork)
- **Product Manager**: 55% KPI (feature delivery, NRR impact), 45% Behavioral (collaboration, communication)
- **VP Sales**: 50% KPI (revenue, pipeline), 50% Behavioral (team development, integrity in deals)

---

### 4. ROLE SCORECARD TEMPLATE

Create a standardized template for evaluating each role.

**Template** (One scorecard per role):

### [Role Name: Sales Executive]

**Role Purpose**: [1 sentence on why this role exists]
Example: "Drive new customer acquisition and expand existing accounts to achieve revenue targets."

**KPI Goals** (60% weight):

| KPI | Definition | Target (Annual) | Weight | Link to M05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New ARR | ARR from new customers | ₹50L | 35% | Sales: New MRR |
| Expansion ARR | ARR from upsells/expansion | ₹20L | 15% | Sales: Expansion revenue |
| Pipeline Coverage | Pipeline value / monthly target | 3x | 15% | Sales: Pipeline |

**Subtotal KPI Score**: [Weighted average of above; scaled to 0-5]

**Example Calculation**:
- New ARR: Achieved ₹45L (90% of target) = 3.6/5, weight 35% = 1.26
- Expansion ARR: Achieved ₹15L (75% of target) = 3.0/5, weight 15% = 0.45
- Pipeline: Achieved 2.8x (93% of target) = 3.7/5, weight 15% = 0.56
- **KPI Score = 1.26 + 0.45 + 0.56 = 2.27 (normalized to 5-pt scale = 3.0/5)**

**Behavioral Goals** (40% weight):

Link behavioral indicators to company values (from {{UPSTREAM_M01}}):

| Value | Behavioral Indicator | Observable Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Customer-First** | "Prioritizes customer success over commission" | Customer CSAT feedback; willingness to take loss-making deals if right fit | Rating: 3 (meets expectations) |
| **Ownership** | "Drives deals to closure; owns entire pipeline" | Deal stage discipline; proactive in overcoming objections; doesn't blame marketing | Rating: 4 (exceeds) |
| **Collaboration** | "Works cross-functionally with CS and product teams" | CS handoff quality; product feedback; team feedback | Rating: 3 (meets) |
| **Integrity** | "Never misrepresents product to close deals" | Customer references; ethics incident count (zero) | Rating: 5 (exceptional) |

**Subtotal Behavioral Score**: [Average of above; scaled to 0-5] = 3.75/5

**Final Performance Rating**:
- KPI Score (3.0) × 60% = 1.80
- Behavioral Score (3.75) × 40% = 1.50
- **Final Rating = 3.3/5 (rounds to 3 = "Meets")**

**Development Plan** (if rating <4):
- Specific behaviors to improve or KPIs to drive
- Support/coaching needed
- Timeline for improvement

### 5. CALIBRATION PROCESS

How ratings are finalized to ensure fairness and consistency.

**Steps**:

1. **Individual Review** (Manager only): Manager rates employee against scorecard in isolation
2. **Peer Input** (Optional): Manager collects feedback from peers/cross-functional colleagues
3. **Calibration Meeting** (Department level): All managers with 3+ direct reports meet to compare ratings
   - Goal: Ensure Rating 4 in Sales = Rating 4 in Product (consistency across departments)
   - Discuss outliers and edge cases
   - Agree on final ratings
4. **Senior Review** (Leadership level): CFO/CEO spot-check sample of ratings for fairness
5. **Communication**: Manager has 1:1 with employee; explains rating, KPI achievement, behavioral feedback

**Calibration Rules**:
- [ ] No rating finalized without manager explaining against scorecard
- [ ] Rating 4+ requires explicit discussion and approval at calibration
- [ ] Rating 2 requires specific evidence and HR involvement
- [ ] Rating variance across departments should not exceed ±0.5

---

### 6. FEEDBACK GUIDELINES

How feedback is delivered and documented.

**Feedback Framework**:

**For High Performers (Rating 4-5)**:
- Emphasize wins and impact
- Explore growth aspirations (promotion path?)
- Offer stretch opportunities or leadership development

**For Solid Performers (Rating 3)**:
- Acknowledge solid contributions
- Identify 1-2 development areas for next period
- Create growth plan if they aspire to promotion

**For Below Expectations (Rating 2)**:
- Be clear on gap between expectation and performance
- Provide specific, behavioral feedback (not vague criticism)
- Create formal improvement plan (PIP) with 30-60-day timeline
- Document everything in writing (HR oversight required)

**Feedback Conversation Template**:
1. **Recap role purpose and context** (1 min)
2. **Scorecard review** (5 min)
3- **KPI discussion**: Achieve vs. target; why the gap or win
   - **Behavioral feedback**: Link to values; specific examples
4. **Overall rating** (1 min)
5. **Development plan** (if <4) (3 min)
6. **Next steps & support** (1 min)

**Documentation**: Record in HR system (Google Sheets, BambooHR, or ADP)

## LINKING TO M01 (VALUES) & M05 (KPIs) & M11 (ORG CHART)

**From M01 (Purpose & Values)**:
- Behavioral indicators map directly to value definitions
- Every value should have 2-3 observable indicators in scorecards
- Values are non-negotiable in evaluation (not just nice-to-have)

**From M05 (KPI Framework)**:
- Role KPIs come from M05 department KPI library
- If M05 defines "New Customer Count = VP Sales KPI," that's on scorecard
- KPI targets cascade from company targets (CEO → Dept → Individual)

**From M11 (Org Chart)**:
- Every role in org chart should have a scorecard
- Scorecard is tied to reporting line (to manager)
- Scorecard should align with role's span of control

**Validation Checklist**:
- [ ] Behavioral indicators reference M01 values
- [ ] KPIs reference M05 library
- [ ] Role scorecard exists for every role in M11
- [ ] Targets cascade from company goals
- [ ] Each person has a written, signed scorecard
